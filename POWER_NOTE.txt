Interpret uncertainty and p-values carefully

The pilot has only three paired training seeds. An exact two-sided sign-flip
test then has a minimum attainable p-value of 2/2^3 = 0.25. It cannot demonstrate
p < 0.05, regardless of the observed effect. Report effect sizes and the
conditional paired bootstrap interval; do not claim statistical significance.

There are 88 candidate-vs-baseline comparisons in the twelve-method,
eight-target/backbone table. The current report applies one conservative Holm
family to all 88. With ten seeds, the minimum raw p is 2/2^10 = 0.001953125,
so the first Holm comparison cannot pass 0.05/88. Thus the default ten-seed
full setting is not sufficient for significance in this complete 88-test family.

Before a genuinely confirmatory study, define a small primary hypothesis family
and perform a power analysis using independent development data, or increase
seeds. For example, fourteen seeds make rejection mathematically possible for
88 tests (minimum p 2/2^14), but mathematical possibility is not adequate power.
Sixteen or more may be needed, depending on effect size/variance. Source-group
independence and realistic household variation matter more than seed counts.
Do not change hypothesis families after looking at confirmatory outcomes.

The bootstrap intervals also condition on the observed dataset and assumed
grouping. Unknown source-window dependencies in the CSV-only pilot are not
resolved by resampling more seeds. No Q1/publication claim is justified by
these p-values, intervals, or a fixed seed count alone.
